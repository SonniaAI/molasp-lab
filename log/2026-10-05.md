### 2. Two corrections to the draft's pruning arithmetic

Both are now enforced by tests so they cannot silently regress.

**Correction A — the n=60 saving is ≈1.5×10², not 10⁴.**
The draft says "(2/1.8393)⁶⁰ ≈ 10⁴" (in both the corrections table and C3
framing). Computed: (2/1.8393)⁶⁰ = 1.0873⁶⁰ = 151.8 ≈ 10²·¹⁸. The draft's
own scaling section says the curves "differ by only 1.087ⁿ", which
*implies* the smaller number; the two passages are inconsistent, and the
smaller one is right. The 10⁴ crossover arrives at **n ≈ 110**, not 60.
Consequence: physical pruning at moderate n is worth ~two orders of
magnitude, not four — this *weakens* the tile path's headline and must be
carried into any C3 mass-scaling experiment design.

**Correction B — pruning buys ≈20 extra variables at the Earth-mass line,
not "roughly ten".**
With branching b = 1.8393, equal mass is reached at
n_b = n₂·log2/log b ≈ 1.137·n₂; at n₂ = 150 that is n ≈ 171 — about **20**
extra variables, twice the draft's headline. This one *strengthens* the
draft's claim (though neither figure changes tractability: 150 → 171 is
still nowhere near clingo's 10⁵–10⁶ variables).

Directional lesson: both errors sit in the draft's pruning enthusiasm — one
overstated its saving (A), one understated it (B). The checkable fix is the
same for both: derive from the branching identity, never from memory.

### 3. `molasp.tightness` — first compiler pass (v0)

The first component of the "what ships first" pipeline: a ground normal
program parser, positive dependency graph, and Fages tightness verdict
(tight ⇔ positive dependency graph acyclic; then supported = stable).
Verified against the draft's canonical counterexamples:

- `p :- p.` → NOT tight (Comp(P) admits {p}; only {} is stable) — cycle `[p]`
- `p :- q. q :- p.` → NOT tight — cycle `[p, q]`
- `a :- not b. b :- not a.` → tight (choice between atoms with no positive
  dependency edges — the gap between this and the self-support case is the
  whole point)

Usage: `python3 -m molasp.tightness program.lp`
