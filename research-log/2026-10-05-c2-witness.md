# 2026-10-05 - The C2 witness: smallest clean supported-but-unstable program

Question (kickoff Q3): what is the smallest non-tight program whose
supported-but-unstable model makes a clean C2 witness?

**Answer: `a. p :- p.`** — 2 atoms, 2 rules, 0 negative literals, one
positive self-loop.

| Program | Stable models | Supported models | Supported-but-unstable |

| `p :- p.` | `{}` | `{}`, `{p}` | `{p}` |
| `a. p :- p.` | `{a}` | `{a}`, `{a,p}` | `{a,p}` |

For `a. p :- p.` the gap is exactly one interpretation, X = {a,p}, with
unfounded set {p}: a has a fact derivation, p has none that does not pass
through p.

## Cleanliness criteria, stated so they can be attacked

A program is a *clean* C2 witness iff:

1. **Non-tight** — a cycle in the positive dependency graph.
2. **A supported-but-unstable X exists.**
3. **X mixes founded and unfounded content** — at least one atom of X with
   a seed-independent derivation (readout positive control) and at least
   one unfounded atom (the disputed signal). A witness whose X is *all*
   unfounded (`p :- p.` alone) cannot distinguish "substrate off" from
   "substrate in the spurious state".
4. **A non-empty stable model** exists to compare decodes against.

`a. p :- p.` meets all four with the least syntax possible:

- One atom cannot satisfy criterion 3 (X ⊆ {p} has no founded content).
- Two atoms need a positive cycle — smallest is the self-loop, one rule —
  plus a fact for the founded atom: two rules. The 2-cycle variant
  (`a. p :- q. q :- p.`) also qualifies but costs a third rule and a
  third atom and carries two unfounded atoms instead of one.

## Why this is the right instance to point Xgrow at

- **The rule `p :- p.` is the entire semantic separator.** Delete it and
  the stable models are unchanged ({a} either way); add it and the
  supported models gain {a,p} (completion moves from p ↔ ⊥ to p ↔ p).
  One rule is the whole difference between the two semantics — the
  experiment is one rule wide.
- **DSD prediction (Path A reading):** dual-rail lowering makes p⁺ an
  autocatalyst — the gate for rule `p :- p` fires on p⁺ and produces p⁺.
  A single leak event self-amplifies, so a well-stirred soup is predicted
  to sit in {a,p}: exactly the supported, non-stable state. a⁺ is the
  built-in positive-control channel.
- **Tile prediction (Path B reading):** in the seeded lowering, p's tile
  has no attachment path from the seed — its only supporting rule
  requires p already placed. Terminal assemblies decode to {a,p} only
  through tile error. C2 then lives or dies on one measurable ratio:
  decode rate of {a,p} versus the kTAM error floor (ε·N scale).

- If the lab later adopts a stronger cleanliness criterion — e.g. the
  cycle must be reachable from a fact through positive bodies — the
  witness becomes `a. p :- q. q :- p.` and `a. p :- p.` drops to
  "separator-only". Recorded here so that choice is explicit, not
  silent.

## What would refute this

1. A strictly smaller program meeting all four criteria (argued
   impossible; attack criterion 3 or the counting argument).
2. A bug in the checker's reduct/least-fixpoint code — bounded, the
   partitions match hand derivations for all five programs.
3. Rejection of the cleanliness criteria themselves — that is a
   legitimate change of question, not a refutation.
