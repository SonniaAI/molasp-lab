## Question

Tick 124 (548234b) priced Bonferroni-Wilson simultaneous per-stratum
bands: family-0.95 simultaneity costs 1.3458× (m=6) / 1.2700× (m=4)
per-stratum width over nominal Wilson. Tick 125 (8611b8c) proved exact
Šidák reclaims only ~0.27%, so that price is irreducible for
independent strata — "the width lever is per-stratum n, not the
correction choice." What does that lever COST? Concretely: how does
the Bonferroni half-width scale with per-stratum n, what n does each
target width require, and is the textbook 1/√n rule safe to budget
against?

## Method

Module `tools/w8_width_vs_n.py` (stdlib-only, tick-123 CI rule). The
planning half-width is the Wilson half-width evaluated AT the design
rate (x/n = p): w(n) = z·√(p(1−p)/n + z²/4n²)/(1 + z²/n) — identical
(by pinned test, 1e-12) to half the reported Wilson interval when
x/n = p. Then:

- **Scaling table**: w at scales s ∈ {1,2,4,8,16} (nᵢ → s·nᵢ) for the
  frozen SIX_WINDOW (m=6, z=2.638257) and CONTESTED_FOUR_ARM (m=4,
  z=2.497705) strata at their observed rates.
- **Halving multiple**: exact n to halve the current width, by
  bisection on the monotone w(n).
- **n-menu**: minimal per-stratum n for half-widths 0.02 / 0.015 /
  0.01 at worst-case p=0.5 and at the worst (max-variance) observed
  frozen rate.
- **Paired-across-scales MC**: the same `random.Random(seed)` uniform
  stream inverted through scale-s tables (seeds 20261013/20261014,
  4000 reps). Scale 1 reproduces the tick-123/124 datasets
  byte-exactly (`first_draw_row` = tick-124 pins
  `[3695,3658,3722,3728,3671,3682]` / `[381,101,396,374]`; Bonferroni
  all-strata coverage reproduces 0.94975 / 0.95275 exactly); scale 4
  is the larger-n sibling of the same experiment.
