# W8 exact conditional test under unequal denominators

Date: 2026-10-10 · SON-4961. **Unreviewed design-calibration output**; this is not a kinetic trajectory result and does not re-analyze the observed six-window outcome.

## Preregistration and method

The question, allocations, seeds and decision rules were frozen at [`de6076c`](https://github.com/SonniaAI/molasp-lab/commit/de6076c9ee3677b310cefc3f31236042fd6783e2) before the full run. Each of three six-window denominator allocations totals 30,000 pair-terminal outcomes: balanced `[5000,5000,5000,5000,5000,5000]`; hot-small `[2500,2500,6250,6250,6250,6250]`; and hot-large `[6500,6500,4250,4250,4250,4250]`. Under each alternative, windows 8 and 9 have `p0+δ`, the other four have `p0`, with `p0=1110/1499` and `δ=.01` or `.02`. Each of nine cells (three global-null allocations plus six alternatives) used 100,000 independent PCG64 datasets with its preregistered seed.

Under the common-probability null, conditional on `K` successes across `N` trials, the successes in any *fixed* two-window subset with `m` trials follow Hypergeometric(`N`, `K`, `m`). The runner enumerates all 15 pairs, computes the conditional upper tail, and rejects only when `15 × min(p_pair) < .05`. Bonferroni bounds familywise error without requiring independence among the pair tests. Unequal denominators change each pair's `m`; therefore the balanced-design shortcut of selecting the two largest raw counts is not used. A regression test pins an unequal-n counterexample where that shortcut picks the wrong pair.

## Results

Null calibration (rejections / 100,000; Wilson 95% Monte Carlo interval): balanced 4,459 (4.459%, [4.333%, 4.589%]); hot-small 4,276 (4.276%, [4.152%, 4.403%]); hot-large 4,359 (4.359%, [4.234%, 4.487%]). These finite simulations are consistent with the analytic Bonferroni cap; they are not the proof of it.

Power under the registered two-hot alternatives:

| Allocation | δ=.01 (Wilson 95%) | δ=.02 (Wilson 95%) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Balanced | 24.872% [24.605%, 25.141%] | 86.553% [86.340%, 86.763%] |
| Hot-small | 14.958% [14.738%, 15.180%] | 62.402% [62.101%, 62.702%] |
| Hot-large | 27.588% [27.312%, 27.866%] | 90.130% [89.944%, 90.313%] |

At the same total of 30,000 observations, placing fewer observations in the two hot windows lowered simulated power versus balanced allocation by 9.914 percentage points at δ=.01 (independent-run Monte Carlo 95% interval [-10.261, -9.567]) and 24.151 points at δ=.02 ([-24.518, -23.784]). Placing more observations in those windows raised power by 2.716 points at δ=.01 ([2.331, 3.101]) and 3.577 points at δ=.02 ([3.296, 3.858]). These contrast intervals quantify Monte Carlo error only. The point is exposure allocation, not a claim that a real experiment should know which windows will be hot.
