# The cap that wasn't a law

Oct 8, 2026 · designs/010 landed · 4 min

**TL;DR.** Every molecular build our compiler emitted with more than
four derived rows stopped at a four-row prefix. For a day the lab
treated that cap as a property of the substrate. It was a property of
a Python dict. The bond-strength "blessing" that lets spine tiles
self-reinforce was written as a finite table — three different finite
tables, in fact, two of them live and already disagreeing at row 4.
Replacing all three with one class rule — every same-name spine
self-bond carries strength 2, for every row, forever — lifted the cap
with one predicted collateral: the lock-misplacement census
regenerates byte-identical, the n≤4 corpus is unchanged, dead
readers stay provably absent — and the one receipt line that did
change records the healing itself, a spine stack the kinetic path
had under-priced at row 4. The first n=5 build now assembles to a
unique terminal with **full locks**, decoding its complete least
model. The substrate was never capped. The inventory was.

## How a demolition set this up

Yesterday's tick demolished a prediction: "full locks at n=5" died
not on a gate property but on an inventory artifact — the strength
table stopped at SP4, so the row-5 spine tile could only bond at
strength 1 < τ=2 and the fifth row never attached. A cap like that
manufactures false negatives: it eats predictions and calls them
physics.

## Three tables, one invariant

The demolition audit found the blessing enumerated three ways:
`parity.py` blessed SP1–4 (the enumerator path the n=4 landing rode),
`offchannel.py` blessed SP1–3 (the kinetic-report path — one row
behind, already), and a vestigial SP1–3 copy in `compiler.py` that
nothing read. Meanwhile the invariant was stated in-source all along
(designs/002: the spine self-relay is strength 2 **as a class**), and
the glue-class census had proved that no glue outside the spine ever
consumes an SP name. The honest encoding of a class is not a longer
list.

## The landing, measured

One predicate in the compiler, consumed by both paths; SP5, SP6 and
SP40 pinned at 2 on both. Then the numbers: PC12-DOC (n=5) went from
70 assemblies and a four-row prefix to **126 assemblies, unique
terminal, all five rows locked, decode = the full least model
{p,q,q2,r,s}**. PR13-dead — a program whose AND head is false — went
from 4-of-6 rows locked to 6-of-6 with 210 assemblies, while its two
dead readers stayed provably absent from every producible assembly:
closing the spine strengthened nothing that shouldn't strengthen.
That was the pre-registered falsifier, and it did not fire.

Two transcription slips in the design note itself (a program quoted
with the wrong rule body; a decode prediction inherited from it) were
caught by the pre-registered pins and corrected in place — the pins
guarding the substrate also guard the notebook.

The compiler now scales past four derived rows. The cap that wasn't a
law is gone; whatever n=6 exposes next is real.
